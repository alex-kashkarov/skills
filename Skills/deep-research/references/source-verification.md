# Source Verification

## Evidence Grades

Grade sources for fitness to the claim, not prestige alone.

- `A`: primary evidence for the claim; strong method or field gold standard.
- `B`: good supporting evidence; limitations do not undermine the claim.
- `C`: usable with caveats.
- `D`: weak; use only if no better source exists and label clearly.
- `F`: do not use as evidence; cite only when critiquing it.

## Evidence Hierarchy

Default hierarchy:

1. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses
2. Randomized controlled trials
3. Controlled non-randomized studies
4. Cohort/case-control studies
5. Systematic reviews of descriptive or qualitative studies
6. Single descriptive, qualitative, archival, or case studies
7. Expert opinion, committee reports, commentary

Adjust by field. In humanities, primary sources or interpretive scholarship can be field-gold-standard evidence. In fast-moving technology or markets, official docs, changelogs, reputable benchmarks, and current industry data may outrank older peer review for current-state claims.

## Verification Checklist

For each cited source:

- Confirm it exists.
- Confirm title, author/organization, date, venue, and URL/DOI where applicable.
- Confirm the source actually supports the cited claim.
- Check whether the source is primary, secondary, or commentary.
- Check for conflicts of interest or institutional agenda.
- Check recency when the claim is current-state or trend-based.
- Prefer exact pages, sections, datasets, tables, or quotes when the claim is precise.

## Reject Conditions

Reject or quarantine a source when:

- The citation cannot be independently found.
- The title/metadata appears fabricated or mashed up.
- The source says something materially different from the claimed support.
- The venue appears predatory and no stronger corroborating source exists.
- The only support is circular citation or unsourced reporting.

## Predatory or Low-Trust Signals

- Fake or unverifiable editorial board
- Suspiciously fast acceptance
- Not indexed where the field expects indexing
- Fake impact metrics
- Aggressive solicitation
- Broad journal scope with low standards
- No ethics or retraction policy
- Undisclosed conflicts of interest

## Citation Discipline

Use short direct quotes only when necessary. Prefer paraphrase plus citation. If a source cannot provide a locator for a precise claim, either weaken the claim or mark the citation as insufficient.
