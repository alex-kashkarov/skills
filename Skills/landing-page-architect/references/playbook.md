# Landing Page Playbook

Use this as the source of truth for creating or auditing product and service landing pages. The goal is a page that is instantly clear, concrete, believable, and safe to act on.

## Minimum Offer Brief

Before writing, establish these facts or make explicit placeholders for missing items:

- **Offer:** what is being sold or offered.
- **Audience:** the specific buyer/user and their situation.
- **Primary action:** buy, start trial, book call, request quote, join waitlist, request access, subscribe, download, or submit inquiry.
- **Outcome:** the concrete result the audience wants.
- **Mechanism:** how the product/service creates the result.
- **Proof available:** testimonials, reviews, metrics, customers, portfolio, sample output, founder expertise, certifications, or case studies.
- **Commitment/risk:** price, time, setup effort, switching cost, privacy/security, contract, refund/cancellation, or uncertainty.

## Complete First-Version Structure

Use this when creating a new product or service landing page and no behavioral data exists yet.

1. **Hero: make the offer instantly legible.** State the offer, audience, outcome, and primary CTA in one plain first-screen message. Use `[Offer] for [audience] who want [result]` when clarity is more important than style. CTA text must reveal the next step, such as `Start free trial`, `Book a 20-min audit`, or `Join the beta`.
2. **Concrete example: make the offer visible.** Show the product UI, product photo, service deliverable, sample output, workflow, report, dashboard, before/after, demo, or real example. This block prevents abstract claims from doing all the work.
3. **Benefits / use cases: make relevance specific.** Give 3-5 reasons the offer matters, tied to real jobs, pains, situations, or desired outcomes. Use `Use it to [specific outcome] when [situation]`. Add a short problem/trigger only when the visitor may not already feel the need.
4. **How it works: make the mechanism understandable.** Explain input, process, and output in 3 steps. Use `You provide [input]. The product/service does [process]. You get [output].` For services, include process and deliverables; for products, include workflow and key details.
5. **Credibility proof: make the claim believable.** Use the strongest honest proof available: testimonial, review, customer/logo, metric, case study, portfolio, certification, founder expertise, usage signal, real artifact, or named quote. Never invent proof.
6. **Decision details: make the buying decision possible.** Add the information a serious visitor needs before acting: pricing or range, trial, cancellation, timeline, setup effort, deliverables, specs, integrations, compatibility, service area, fit/not-fit criteria, support, or requirements.
7. **Risk reducers / FAQ: make action feel safe.** Answer real objections about cost, time, privacy, security, quality, support, refund, guarantee, implementation, switching, data access, or what happens after the click. Avoid fake promotional FAQs.
8. **Final CTA: make the next step obvious.** Repeat the same primary CTA with reassurance, such as `Book a call - no prep required` or `Start free - no credit card required`.

## Adaptation Rules

- Move **concrete example** higher for abstract, AI, technical, new-category, or service offers.
- Move **credibility proof** and **risk reducers** higher for high-ticket, sensitive, B2B, professional-service, or trust-heavy offers.
- Add **problem/trigger** when visitors may not recognize the need or cost of the status quo.
- Add **differentiation/comparison** when visitors are likely comparing alternatives, vendors, categories, or current workarounds.
- Show **pricing/commitment** earlier when hiding it creates anxiety. If pricing is custom, explain the pricing path and what happens before a quote.
- Shorten the page when the action is low commitment, traffic is high intent, or the offer is already obvious.
- Expand proof, decision details, and risk reducers when the offer is expensive, unfamiliar, custom, sensitive, or requires switching.

## Offer-Type Emphasis

- **Product pages:** prioritize product reality, benefits, details, pricing/commitment, reviews, support, compatibility, and risk reducers.
- **Service pages:** prioritize trust, process, deliverables, timeline, fit/not-fit, case proof, pricing expectations, and next-step clarity.
- **AI pages:** show input-output examples, quality controls, limits, data/privacy cues, and real outputs; do not lead with generic AI claims.
- **Developer-tool pages:** show code, API, docs, install path, demo, integrations, reliability, and technical proof early.
- **High-ticket pages:** move proof, process, commitment clarity, and risk reducers higher than on low-ticket pages.
- **Lead-gen pages:** make the lead magnet, consultation, audit, or quote request valuable and explain exactly what happens after submission.

## Bottleneck Structures

Use these when improving an existing page or when a first version has one known risk.

### Orientation Failure

Use when visitors may not understand the offer quickly. Structure: **Hero** with literal offer/audience/result/CTA; **Concrete example** that makes the offer visible; **Benefits/use cases** that show relevance; **Credibility cue** that proves legitimacy; **CTA** that repeats the same action clearly.

### Tangibility Gap

Use when visitors understand the promise but cannot picture the offer. Structure: **Hero** with outcome/audience/CTA; **Demo/sample/deliverable** showing the actual thing; **How it works** in input/process/output steps; **Before-after or use case** showing transformation; **Proof** that the mechanism works; **CTA** matched to the demonstrated outcome.

### Commitment Friction

Use when visitors seem interested but hesitate to act. Structure: **Hero** with offer/result/CTA; **Proof near top** to reduce skepticism; **What happens next** after clicking or submitting; **Price/commitment cue** to reduce anxiety; **Risk reducers/FAQ** for real objections; **CTA** with reassurance.

## Block Quality Checks

- **Hero:** a stranger can say what the offer is, who it is for, and why it matters within 10 seconds.
- **Concrete example:** the visual/example carries information, not decoration.
- **Benefits/use cases:** each benefit names a real job, situation, or outcome.
- **How it works:** the visitor can picture the steps and final output.
- **Proof:** claims are supported by specific, honest evidence.
- **Decision details:** pricing, process, scope, setup, or requirements are not hidden when they affect action.
- **Risk reducers/FAQ:** objections are real and answered plainly.
- **CTA:** the visitor knows what will happen next.

## Copy Rules

- Use plain nouns before clever phrasing.
- Put concrete words at the start of headings and bullets.
- Replace vague claims with specific outcomes, mechanisms, or proof.
- Replace fake FAQs with real objections.
- Use one primary CTA; allow a secondary CTA only if it reduces friction without competing.
- Remove blocks that do not create one of these beliefs: understand, relevance, tangibility, belief, safety, next step.
